---
title: "What two hackathon wins taught me about building fast"
date: "03/2026"
excerpt: "Winning Best Neuro Hack at IrvineHacks and Best Healthcare App at Webjam back-to-back taught me more about product thinking than any class."
---
<p>Two hackathons. Two wins. A lot of sleep deprivation and a lot of lessons.</p>

<p>At IrvineHacks 2026, our team built PocketZot — a browser extension with a Shimeji-style anteater mascot that walks around AI chat sites and reacts to how you prompt. We fine-tuned a GPT-4o-mini model on a cognitive offloading taxonomy and built out a full React popup with a shop and reward economy. 36 hours. Best Neuro Hack.</p>

<p>At ICSSC Webjam 2025, we built Zotletics (then called Anteater Gym Service) — a fitness platform that generates personalized workout plans based on the actual equipment at each UCI campus gym. Best Healthcare App.</p>

<p>The common thread between both wins: we built something that felt real. Not a demo, not a concept — something you could actually use. That shift in mindset from "make something impressive" to "make something useful" is the biggest thing I'm taking away. Judges feel the difference immediately.</p>

<p>The other lesson: scope is everything. At a hackathon you have to cut features ruthlessly and nail the core loop. I've gotten much better at that.</p>
