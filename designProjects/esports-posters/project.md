<p class="project-detail-lead"><strong>TuHS Esports Team Posters</strong> ran across a couple of years, in two phases. The first was recruitment, right after the sport got funding. We had no identity and no main logo. The second phase locked to Esport Wolf red, the same red as the logo, the jersey, the merch, and everything digital. I ran the team Instagram at <a href="https://www.instagram.com/tuhsesports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">@tuhsesports</a>.</p>

<h2 class="project-detail-h2">Getting on the wall</h2>
<p>We only had about a dozen poster boards. The halls were already covered, so if a poster did not read from across the hall it might as well not be up. These three were Valorant, Apex Legends, and Smash Bros. Different games, different layouts. The job was to get people in the room.</p>

<h2 class="project-detail-h2">Rainbow Six nationals</h2>
<p>The Rainbow Six team went to nationals in the HSEL tournament. We set up a watch event so people could come see the players compete live on a stage. The first poster used a photo of a player at his setup. They wanted a different photo, so the next pass used that. I also made another poster and presented the two together. They liked that one most, and that is the one that went up.</p>

<h2 class="project-detail-h2">Esport Wolf red</h2>
<p>Once the brand was red and black, the posters followed. Two colors. The wolf, the HSEL mark, the game huge behind the character. That shared layout was the start of the branding guide. Color was the easy part. What mattered was that they popped off a wall already littered with posters. Esport Wolf red is hard to miss from the other end of the hall.</p>
