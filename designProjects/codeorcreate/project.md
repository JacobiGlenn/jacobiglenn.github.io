<p class="project-detail-lead"><strong>Tio</strong> is short for "Travel in Oregon". I worked on a team of 3 as a designer during the 2024 codeORcreate event, Oregon's regional programming challenge. The project is a transit app for daily riders and people with disabilities that make commuting difficult. We wanted to focus on these people specifically because apps like Google Maps don't account for their unique struggles in meaningful ways </p>


<div style="margin:0 0 0.25rem;">
  <span class="project-award">&#127942; Best Presentation — codeORcreate 2024</span>
</div>

<p><a href="https://www.oregon.gov/odot/planning/state-of-the-system/pages/mobility.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ODOT's 2023 Transportation Needs and Issues Survey</a> gave a lot of helpful information when it came to this problem. According to their survey, Fifty-two percent of people said they would take transit if the service near them got better. Forty-two percent said they would walk or bike if the connections were better. Nearly a fifth of Oregon households already have someone on transit at least once a week, and nine percent of people old enough to drive don't have a license. The desire and userbase of public transit is there, and the trimet app just isn't cutting it. </p>
<br>
<p>Public transit apps only tell you the next bus. Tio is supposed to plan the ride around the person and their specific needs. A wheelchair route that makes sure the user stays on buses that can board them and keeps the walk short to limit physical excertion. A week of bus routes integrated into their Google Calendar so the app works with your daily workflow. A pass and a ticket in the same wallet for ease of use. When a bus is late, the planned ticket cancels and the app reroutes. </p>

<h2 class="project-detail-h2">Home</h2>
<p>Search where you want to go. Our example is Tigard, Oregon (which is a town but imagine it's a more specific location). 
<br>
<br>
Under that, the nearest lines are listed: which bus already left, which one is coming, and how long ago (These show up BEFORE pressing the search button to give an at-a-glance analysis of what is nearby). 
<br>
<br>
The map sits below the list, with an imported open-source map software.
<br></p>

<h2 class="project-detail-h2">Accesibility Calculation</h2>
<p>Once you press search the routing screen comes up. Direct, efficient, and cheapest, each with a time and an accessibility score out of five. These three options are also supposed to be as simple as possible for our older users to make ease of use within the app. Direct to Tigard was 38 minutes and five stars. Efficient was 24 minutes and three. Cheapest was 52 minutes and two.<br></p>

<h2 class="project-detail-h2">Rider Profile</h2>
<p>Profile section shows the rider and the weeks work schedule. Work days in blue, days off in yellow. Import the week into Google Calendar or from your google calendar, whichever supports YOUR workflow. Rides are automatically booked based off of your work schedule, or you can manually book. Commute times are also listed in your google calendar for easy planning of your week.<br></p>

<h2 class="project-detail-h2">Expansive settings menu</h2>
<p>Settings is where the route calculations take place. Wheelchair friendly routing, text to speech (for blind friendly), voice control, and an option to strip images (so the text to speech doesn't get bogged up). Dark mode, a font size slider, and English, Spanish, French, or Chinese. The proposal called for certified translators, and that is what we had available.</p>
<br>
<p> The settings would help edit the way your routing worked and we planned to add a lot of features within onboarding to get a good look at what the users direct needs are.<br> </p>

<p> This project ended up winning "Best Presentation" at codeORcreate 2024 and we pitched it more as an agrigator of multiple services. I worked as a lead designer and chose a more minimalistic approach to make it easy for elderly to understand and to appeal to a blind audience (which was a large portion of our user base). I also worked on our google slideshow presentation and was the main presentor during the event. 
<br>
<br>
We pitched the product to a panel of 3 judges who ran successful businesses in the Oregon metropolitan area, all three said they'd invest in the product if it were to launch today.</p>
