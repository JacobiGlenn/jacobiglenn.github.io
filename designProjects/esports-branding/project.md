<p class="project-detail-lead"><strong>Esports Branding</strong> is the Tualatin High School esports identity. I was one of the designers in the digital arts department, so when the school went through a branding change I ended up on a lot of it. The logo came first, through most of junior year and again around November and December of senior year, after the mascot changed. Once that mark settled, the Instagram stayed red and black: match highlights, videos, and promo, usually more than five posts a week. The same look went on a drink we made with the on-campus cafe, Wolf Cafe, to bring in more revenue. Buy the drink, get an exclusive sticker.</p>

<h2 class="project-detail-h2">Half wolf, half tech</h2>
<p>Once the school had a new wolf, I split the face: one half the mascot, the other half circuitry. I thought the esports head coach would like that. He wanted something simpler that would sit on a jersey and iron on cleanly, and he liked how the wolf-head shield read. So we dropped the extra parts and kept an outline of the wolf with techno eyes.</p>

<h2 class="project-detail-h2">Team posters</h2>
<p>The posters ran across the same years, in two phases. The first was recruitment, right after the sport got funding. We had no identity and no main logo. The second phase locked to Esport Wolf red, the same red as the logo, the jersey, the merch, and everything digital. I ran the team Instagram at <a href="https://www.instagram.com/tuhsesports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">@tuhsesports</a>.</p>

<h2 class="project-detail-h2">Esport Wolf red</h2>
<p>Once the brand was red and black, the posters followed. Two colors. The wolf, the HSEL mark, the game huge behind the character. That shared layout was the start of the branding guide. Color was the easy part. What mattered was that they popped off a wall already littered with posters. Esport Wolf red is hard to miss from the other end of the hall.</p>
